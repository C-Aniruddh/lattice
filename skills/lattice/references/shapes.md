# Choosing the shape

Eight shapes. Match the sentence to one, announce it in a line, and go. **Do not ask.** A user
who typed a sentence has already told you everything they know how to tell you; a menu is you
handing the work back.

Match on what the sentence is *about*, not on the words it happens to use. If two shapes fit,
take the one whose first screen is prettier — you can add the other half later, and the first
minute is the part that decides whether they stay.

If nothing matches, take **lit**. It is the best-looking first frame in the kit and almost any
idea can be told through it.

---

| shape | the sentence sounds like | load, in this order | the first screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| **lit** | lighthouse, lamp, torch, beacon, fog, cave, night, dark, "push back the…" | `starting` `art` `world` `hud`, plus `economy` if the light costs something | dark terrain, one light with a visible edge, something drifting |
| **build** | town, city, farm, base, factory, colony, port, "build", "place", "grow a…" | `starting` `art` `world` `input` `hud` | terrain, and a translucent ghost following the pointer |
| **idle** | idle, incremental, tycoon, clicker, "while I'm away", money, resources, upgrades | `starting` `art` `economy` `hud` `saving` | one producer, a number rolling up, one button with a price |
| **crowd** | villagers, ants, traffic, commuters, pilgrims, a queue, "hundreds of…" | `starting` `art` `world` `hud` `performance` | a road with two hundred things already walking it |
| **terrain** | island, canyon, mountain, river, erosion, dig, sculpt, flood, "the land" | `starting` `art` `world` `performance` | a heightfield under a moving sun |
| **explore** | endless, infinite, wander, roam, procedural, "no edge", "keeps going" | `starting` `art` `world` `performance` | terrain minting itself under a panning camera |
| **listen** | music, sound, rhythm, hum, chord, tune, instrument, "by ear" | `starting` `art` `sound` `hud` | a lit board humming one note, one thing answering |
| **story** | rpg, quest, adventure, npc, talk, errand, inventory, "a little world with people" | `starting` `art` `world` `input` `hud` `saving` | a village, and a person you can send somewhere |

`traps` is not on any row. Read it whenever something works and looks wrong, which will be
often.

## Worked examples

Read these for the register of the announcement line — a picture of their game, in their words,
in about twenty.

**"a game where you rebuild a lighthouse and the light pushes back the fog"** → **lit**
(`starting` `art` `world` `hud` `economy` — rebuilding costs something, so there is an economy).

> Building you a lit world: a fogged coast, a lighthouse you rebuild course by course, and a
> beam that pushes the fog back further as it grows.

**"a little town where people walk around"** → **crowd** (not `build` — the sentence is about
the people, and a hundred walkers is a better first screen than an empty plot).

> Building you a valley town with a road through it and a couple of hundred people already
> walking it. You'll be able to add to the road.

**"an idle game about bees"** → **idle**.

> Building you a hive: honey ticking up on its own, more bees to buy as it does, and it keeps
> earning while the tab is shut.

**"minecraft"** → **terrain**. The sentence names something the kit cannot be, so take the part
it can — the land as a toy — and say what you are giving them without saying what you are not.

> Building you a world you can shape: raise a ridge, cut a channel, and watch the water and the
> trees settle into it.

**"i dunno, something cool"** → **lit**, and no further questions.

> Building you a cave with torches in it — dark, and the light pools where you put them. Say
> the word if you'd rather have something else.

## What the shape is not

It is **not** a promise about what the finished game is. It picks the first screen, the
specialist skills you load, and the order you build in. Everything after the first minute is
driven by their sentence and by whatever they say when they see it.

It is **not** a template to copy verbatim. Two `lit` games should not look alike; the palette,
the massing, the thing that moves and what the verb is all come from the sentence.

And it is **not** worth agonizing over. A wrong shape costs a few minutes and is fixed by them
saying "actually…". A question costs you the only thing this whole design is protecting.
